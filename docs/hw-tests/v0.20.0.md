# v0.20.0: DMR receive and transmit

Digital channels receive and transmit DMR through a repeater or direct.

Receive: the transceiver tunes with the radio's digital set of values
and the baseband decodes on the channel's colour code. Through a
repeater only the channel's slot is heard; direct, both. A call is heard
only when it is meant for the radio: the channel's talkgroup, the one
chosen with `dmr tg`, a talkgroup in the channel's receive group list,
the radio's own ID or all-call; other calls are logged once as `not for
us` and stay muted. The vocoder's speaker gain comes from the
calibration. A heard call takes the whole screen: call type and slot,
the caller's callsign (or contact name, or ID) with the user's name and
ID from the codeplug's user directory, the talkgroup, and the call's
time; it stays dimmed for five seconds after the call.

Transmit: PTT calls the channel's contact (or `dmr tg <id>` /
`dmr pc <id>`) from the channel's radio ID (or `dmr id <id>`). Through a
repeater the radio uses the repeater's timing when it has it, waits for
it while the repeater is on the air, and otherwise sends one wake-up
block, at most twice, as ETSI TS 102 361-1 describes. When it
transmits, the transceiver adds no deviation of its own and keeps its digital filter setting, the baseband modulates with
its calibrated gain, and the amplifier follows the baseband's slot line,
its output ramped with each slot at High and Turbo. Release sends the
terminator. A refused PTT shows its reason on the screen. The codeplug's
"enhance audio" setting shapes the transmitted voice.

## Before you start

- The log port open.
- A DMR repeater in reach with traffic on a talkgroup in the channel's
  receive group list, and some on one that is not, and a way to see the
  network's last heard list.
- A codeplug with a digital channel for it (contact and radio ID) and the
  user directory (callsign database) written.

## Steps

| # | Do | Expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Flash; pick the digital channel | "DeltaMM 0.20.0"; `dmr: receiving ...`; no `crash:` |
| 2 | Wait for the repeater's beacon | Timing found; bursts of the channel's slot only |
| 3 | A call on the channel's talkgroup | The call screen: `GROUP CALL`, the caller's callsign large, name and ID under it, the talkgroup's name and number; the time runs; clear speech |
| 4 | The call ends | `LAST GROUP CALL`, dimmed; standby after five seconds |
| 5 | During a call, press a key | Back to standby at once; the call stays audible |
| 6 | A call on a talkgroup not in the list | Log `call from ... to ...: not for us`; no audio, no call screen |
| 7 | Repeater idle, PTT on the channel | `repeater wake-up 1: answered`, `transmitting ...`; the network's last heard list shows the radio's ID on the talkgroup |
| 8 | PTT and speak | The other station hears clear speech |
| 9 | Release | `slot line rose N times, slots keyed N`, `transmit off (PTT released)`; the repeater ends the call at once |
| 10 | Repeat 8 at Low, Mid, High and Turbo | Clear at every level |
| 11 | PTT while another call is heard | Notice `Channel busy`; nothing sent |
| 12 | PTT on a receive-only channel | Notice `Receive only`; nothing sent |
| 13 | During a call, talk loudly close to the microphone, then quietly | Equally clear both ways: no analog voice rides on the DMR signal |
| 14 | Codeplug "enhance audio" on, restart | `baseband: voice enhancement on` |
| 15 | FM channel: PTT | FM transmit as before |
| 16 | Read the latest uptime line | Every task above 50 unused stack words |

Findings:

- The baseband's demodulated signal reaches it with reversed polarity on
  this board; it decodes nothing until told so.
- Before it has received a repeater's timing the baseband takes no
  frame for a given slot; only "no slot", on its own timing, works, which
  is how ETSI TS 102 361-1 lets a radio wake an idle repeater.
- On a digital transmission the transceiver's microphone input stays
  open, but its deviation must be zero, so the transceiver adds no FM of
  its own; the baseband modulates the RF. With the calibrated FM
  deviation left in, analog voice rode on the DMR signal.
- A repeater wake-up keys the amplifier continuously. The slot line's
  interrupt switched the transmitter off on every edge when slot keying
  was not active, which cut the wake-up block the moment the baseband
  sent it; that alone kept the repeater silent until fixed.
- The codeplug marks contacts in use with a clear bit, unlike its other
  lists.
