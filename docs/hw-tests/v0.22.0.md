# v0.22.0: GPS receiver

The radio's GPS receiver works: on or off with the codeplug's setting or
Menu, GPS; a GPS screen from Menu, GPS, Status; a status bar mark and
console commands. The receiver sends NMEA on USART1 at 9600 baud; its
supply runs through the FM transceiver.

## Before you start

- A view of the sky: outdoors, or at a window. A first fix can take a few
  minutes.
- The log port open in a terminal. Unplug the USB cable to listen or
  talk: in the accessory jack it cuts the speaker and the microphone.

## Steps

| # | Do | Expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Flash; Menu, GPS | Two items: GPS (as the codeplug sets it) and Status (Off) |
| 2 | GPS: On | Log `gps: on` and `gps: receiver power on`; back in the list, Status Searching; a grey GPS mark in the status bar |
| 3 | Console `gps raw on`, then `gps raw off` after a few seconds | Sentences such as `$GNRMC,...` and `$GNGGA,...` in the log, about one burst a second; then they stop |
| 4 | Wait with a view of the sky | Log `gps: fix: <lat>, <lon>, <alt> m, <n> of <m> satellites, <time> UTC`; the mark turns green, Status shows Fix |
| 5 | Choose Status | The GPS screen: "Fix", position, altitude, satellites, speed, UTC time and date matching a clock; the seconds count up without the screen flashing |
| 6 | Menu on the GPS screen, then GPS: Off, then On, then Status | Menu goes back to the list without switching anything; the screen then shows "Searching" and later "Fix", replacing each other cleanly; log `gps: off`, `gps: on` |
| 7 | Exit | The GPS list, its Status matching the screen |
| 8 | Console `gps` | Bytes and sentences counted; 0 bad and 0 lost, or a handful |
| 9 | Receive and transmit FM on a channel with the GPS on, USB unplugged | As without GPS: clear audio both ways |
| 10 | Hold Exit on the standby screen a few times | Standby, log and key test screens only |
| 11 | Switch the radio off and on | The GPS starts as the codeplug sets it |

## If something is off

- Step 2 logs `no data from the receiver for 5 s`: the receiver sends
  nothing at 9600 baud on PA3. Note the line and run `gps` for the counts.
- Step 3 shows garbled text: the baud rate differs.

# GPS receiver setup

Once the GPS receiver answers after power-on, DeltaMM asks it for RMC, GGA
and GSV only, once a second, and for the satellite systems the codeplug or
Menu, GPS, Systems chooses. The console talks to the receiver with
`gps query`, `gps restart` and `gps send`.

## Before you start

- The log port open in a terminal.
- A view of the sky for the last steps.

## Steps

| # | Do | Expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Flash; console `gps on` | Log `gps: receiver set up: <systems>` within a second or two, then the receiver's own TXT lines (maker, module, chip, firmware) |
| 2 | `gps raw on` for a few seconds, then `gps raw off` | Only GGA, GSV and RMC, once a second; no antenna TXT line |
| 3 | `gps query` | One TXT line with the receiver's firmware version |
| 4 | Menu, GPS, Systems: GPS | `gps: receiver set up: GPS`; with `gps raw on` the sentences start with `$GP`, no `$BDGSV` |
| 5 | Systems: GPS + BeiDou | `$GN` sentences and a `$BDGSV` line |
| 6 | Menu, GPS: Status shows "Searching", then "Fix" | The whole word, not cut short |
| 7 | Wait for the uptime line | The gps task keeps well over 100 unused stack words |
| 8 | `gps restart cold`, then `gps raw on` | The receiver's TXT lines again, the fix is lost and later found again; it still sends only GGA, GSV and RMC from the chosen systems |
| 9 | Switch the radio off and on | GPS and its systems as the codeplug sets them |

## Results

2026-10-26: all steps pass. The receiver reports CASIC, ATGM336H, AT6558,
firmware URANUS5 V5.3.0.0; after setup only GGA, GSV and RMC arrive, and
the settings survive a cold restart. The gps task keeps 280 unused stack
words.

# Real-time clock

The radio keeps UTC on its battery-backed real-time clock, a GPS fix
corrects it, and the status bar shows the local time from the codeplug's
time zone or Menu, GPS and time, Zone. The console has `time` and
`time set`.

## Before you start

- The log port open in a terminal, and a PC whose clock is right.
- A view of the sky for the GPS steps.

## Steps

| # | Do | Expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Flash; watch the log | `clock: <date> <time> UTC` close to the PC's UTC (a clock that was never set: `time unknown`) |
| 2 | Watch the status bar | The local time (HH:MM) left of the volume: UTC plus the codeplug's time zone |
| 3 | Menu, GPS and time, Zone: pick another zone | The status bar follows at once; the menu shows e.g. `UTC+2:00` |
| 4 | `time set` a time 60 s behind the PC's UTC | `clock: set from the console: ... (was 60 s ahead)`; the status bar follows |
| 5 | `gps on` and wait for a fix | `clock: set from GPS: <the PC's UTC> (was 60 s behind)` |
| 6 | Switch the radio off for a minute, then on | Step 1's line again, still matching the PC within a second or two |
| 7 | Flash the factory firmware and look at its clock; flash DeltaMM back | The same time in both: the counter and its marker carry over |

## Results

2026-10-27: all steps pass. After flashing and after a minute switched
off, the clock read the PC's UTC within a second; the factory firmware
showed the same time. Changing the zone from UTC+3:00 to UTC+2:00 moved
the status bar at once, and a GPS fix corrected a clock set 60 s behind
to the PC's time.

# GPS power saving

With battery save on, the GPS receiver runs only until it has a fix, stays
on ten seconds more, and sleeps until the next fix is due: once an hour
for the clock, at once while the GPS screen is open. It wakes early by
the time its last start took, plus a quarter. A start without a fix is
given up after five minutes. With battery save off, the receiver stays on.
The status bar's GPS mark is amber while the receiver sleeps.

## Before you start

- The log port open in a terminal, and a PC whose clock is right.
- A view of the sky.
- GPS on in the codeplug or the menu.

## Steps

| # | Do | Expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Menu, Battery: off; flash or restart | `gps: power saving off`, `gps: receiver on`; the receiver stays on after its fix |
| 2 | Menu, Battery: On | `gps: power saving on`, then `gps: receiver asleep; wakes in <a little under 3600> s`; the GPS mark turns amber, Menu, GPS and time, Status reads `Sleeping` |
| 3 | Console `gps` | `receiver asleep, sleeps between fixes, wakes in <n> s; shortest need 3600 s, start lead <n> s` |
| 4 | Open the GPS screen (Menu, GPS and time, Status) | `gps: receiver on`; the screen shows `Searching`, then `Fix` |
| 5 | Leave the GPS screen | `gps: receiver asleep; wakes in <n> s`; the mark turns amber |
| 6 | `gps need 300`; after the fix and sleep that follows, `time set` a time 60 s behind the PC's UTC and wait without touching the radio | `gps: console needs a position every 300 s`; about five minutes after the last fix, less the start lead: `gps: receiver on for a fix`, a fix, `clock: set from GPS: <the PC's UTC> (was 60 s behind)`, and sleep again ten seconds after the fix. `gps need 0` ends it |
| 7 | Indoors far from a window, switch the radio off and on | `gps: receiver on for a fix`; five minutes later without a fix, `gps: receiver asleep; wakes in 3600 s` |
| 8 | Menu, GPS: off while asleep | `gps: off`; no mark; no wake later |
| 9 | Console `save 0`, then `save 1` | Menu, Battery follows (Off, then On); `gps: power saving off` and the receiver on, then `power saving on` and asleep after its next fix |

## Results

2026-10-27: all steps pass. Indoors with GPS only, starts reached a fix
in 35 to 105 s and the receiver slept ten seconds after each; the learnt
start lead was 44 to 131 s. A start without a fix was given up after five
minutes and slept 3600 s. With `gps need 300`, the receiver woke by itself
223 s after sleeping, had a fix 66 s later, the second it was due, and
corrected a clock set 60 s behind.
